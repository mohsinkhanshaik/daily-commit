# AI Pulse - 2026-11-07

A week-ending session where a chip-stock rout, a new federal AI crime unit and a gated cyber model all landed within 48 hours.

## Nasdaq AI-chip rout erases a record 2.3 trillion dollars
The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4 percent on Thursday and the Philadelphia semiconductor index dropped 12 percent, with Nvidia alone losing roughly 450 billion dollars in market value. Bloomberg reports prospective Anthropic IPO buyers now discuss a 1.2 to 1.3 trillion dollar valuation, down from 1.4 to 1.5 trillion a week ago and 2 trillion in October. Why it matters: public markets are repricing AI infrastructure risk, and that reset flows straight into private valuations and IPO timing.
Source: CNBC (https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/qualcomm-earnings-chip-stocks-election.html)

## Voters and cities push back on data centers
Data-center restrictions passed in 40 of 41 local ballot measures after the U.S. midterms, and authorities from New York to Amsterdam tightened siting rules. OpenAI responded by promising more transparency with local officials and ending mandatory nondisclosure agreements for public officials. Why it matters: local permission, not chips, is becoming the binding constraint on new compute capacity.
Source: Reuters (https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/where-authorities-are-restricting-data-centres-amid-ai-boom-2026-11-05/)

## DOJ launches a Super Intelligence Task Force
Attorney General Todd Blanche announced a unit to prosecute unlawful uses of advanced AI by foreign governments, criminal groups and fraudsters, absorbing the AI Litigation Task Force set up in January 2026. The same week DOJ charged three people over an AI voice-clone scheme that impersonated executives at seven organizations and laundered more than 129 million dollars into crypto. Why it matters: AI misuse is moving from guidance documents to dedicated federal prosecutors.
Source: U.S. Department of Justice (https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-launches-super-intelligence-task-force)

## OpenAI gates GPT-6 Cyber behind a Critical risk rating
OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber only to vetted Daybreak Red organizations after rating its cyber capability Critical; it scores 48.5 percent on ExploitGym versus 42.4 percent for GPT-6 Astra, found 81 novel Firefox bugs and produced working exploits for 948 Linux kernel issues. Separately, Check Point documented an AI-agent campaign that stole more than 300 iOS signing certificates for an estimated 700 to 1,500 dollars. Why it matters: offensive capability is now cheap on both sides, so access control has become the main safety lever.
Source: CyberScoop (https://cyberscoop.com/openai-gpt-6-cyber-security-model/)

## METR says Anthropic AI R and D is running about twice as fast
METR's assessment of internal data from August 12 to October 29 finds Anthropic's benchmark scores growing 1.9 times faster since October 2025, with a 95 percent interval of 1.5 to 2.8 times, and attributes about 80 percent of the speedup to AI tooling. Most of the roughly 100-page report's key evidence is redacted at Anthropic's request. Why it matters: it is the first outside estimate that AI is measurably accelerating its own development at a frontier lab.
Source: METR (https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/)

## Open-weight mixture-of-experts models keep arriving
Thinking Machines launched Inkling 2 Small, a 594-billion-parameter model with 17 billion active that scores 86.3 percent on SWEBench Pro Hard, with Apache 2.0 weights promised next week. Baidu open-sourced ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base at 280 billion parameters and about 14 billion active, trained on 33.5 trillion tokens. Why it matters: sparse models with under 20 billion active parameters are making near-frontier coding cheap to serve and self-host.
Source: Thinking Machines (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)

## Google commits 17 billion dollars to a Wyoming campus
Google's Project Tembo in Laramie County will draw on a planned 702-megawatt Bloom Energy fuel-cell development with carbon capture, while Microsoft declined to renew its 2023 Oracle cloud contract for Bing AI capacity and is reassessing more than 80 billion dollars of outside-cloud commitments. Why it matters: hyperscalers are moving toward self-built, self-powered capacity even as they prune third-party deals.
Source: Google (https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/wyoming-data-center/)

Trend watch: the policy center of gravity is shifting from Washington rulemaking to prosecutors, courts and local ballots, at the same moment markets question the capex that all of this regulation is reacting to.
