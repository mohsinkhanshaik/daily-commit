# AI Pulse - 2026-11-06

Chip geopolitics tighten around Nvidia while labs push security automation, giant open-weight models and new acquisitions.

## Nvidia reportedly imposes a 51 percent non-China rule on cloud partners
Nvidia has verbally told cloud providers buying its chips that at least 51 percent of their services must go to customers outside China, per a Financial Times report, applying to new contracts worldwide. Chinese firms such as Alibaba and ByteDance have rented Nvidia capacity in Singapore and Malaysia, and Nvidia's share of China's data center AI chip market already fell to 34 percent in the first half of 2026 per IDC. Why it matters: a vendor-enforced rule could close the offshore rental route faster than formal export controls.
Source: TheStreet (https://www.thestreet.com/crypto/artificial-intelligence/nvidias-new-51-rule-could-shut-chinas-backdoor-to-ai-chips)

## About 600,000 H200 chips pile up in Hong Kong awaiting Beijing approval
Roughly 600,000 Nvidia H200 chips are reportedly stored in Hong Kong while China withholds large-volume import approval, with several big internet companies receiving only about 10,000 each so far. Grey-market eight-card H200 servers were offered for more than 6 million yuan (about 900,000 dollars) at the end of October, roughly triple the price a year earlier. Why it matters: Beijing, not Washington, is now the bottleneck on approved US chip sales into China.
Source: Express Tribune (https://tribune.com.pk/story/2638831/nvidia-chips-pile-up-in-hong-kong-as-china-withholds-import-approval)

## OpenAI pipeline finds and exploits hundreds of Linux kernel bugs
OpenAI researchers report an autonomous security pipeline that flagged 2,308 potential Linux kernel flaws, reproduced 1,724 and built working exploits for 948 local privilege-escalation bugs for about 1.21 million dollars in API credits. Maintainers had patched 61 upstream with 43 more in progress. Why it matters: automated vulnerability discovery is now cheaper than human triage capacity, shifting the bottleneck to patching.
Source: OpenAI (https://openai.com/index/a-thousand-fewer-paths-to-root/)

## Moonshot AI ships Kimi K3.1 Pro at 2.8 trillion parameters
Moonshot AI released Kimi K3.1 Pro, a 2.8-trillion-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts model with a 1-million-token context window that activates about 104 billion parameters per request. Tencent also launched Hy4, a 770-billion-parameter model with chart reading and computer-use abilities. Why it matters: Chinese labs keep scaling open models despite chip constraints, leaning on sparse activation to stay efficient.
Source: Hugging Face (https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)

## Meta acquires AI coding startup Conductor
Meta bought Conductor, a startup whose tool splits software projects across multiple coding agents, and Conductor will shut its cloud service on November 20. Terms were not disclosed. Why it matters: multi-agent coding orchestration is becoming a capability big platforms would rather own than partner for.
Source: TechCrunch (https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/meta-acquires-ai-coding-startup-conductor/)

## Apple trims internal AI usage budgets
Apple cut some engineers' daily internal AI allowances from 300 to 200 dollars, with cuts to 100 dollars under consideration and some non-engineering teams possibly dropping to 25 dollars. Why it matters: even the richest companies are now metering agentic AI spend per employee, a signal that inference cost is a real line item.
Source: Bloomberg (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/apple-cuts-internal-ai-usage-budgets-in-move-to-reduce-spending)

## Future Secure AI raises 80 million dollars at a 1.2 billion dollar valuation
Austin-based Future Secure AI, which builds AI co-workers, raised 80 million US dollars in convertible notes at a 1.2 billion dollar valuation from investors including Square Peg, Macquarie Asset Management and Firetrail. It is the company's third round; the previous one closed in June 2025. Why it matters: capital keeps flowing to agent products that sell outcomes rather than model access.
Source: Startup Daily (https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/funding/future-secure-ai-raises-us80-million-for-ai-co-workers/)

Trend watch: compute access is turning into a negotiated, vendor-policed resource while AI spend gets metered inside companies, so efficiency stories like sparse models and cheap automated security work keep gaining weight.
