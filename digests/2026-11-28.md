# AI Pulse - 2026-11-28
Thanksgiving-week wrap: chip curbs bite across Asia, OpenAI resets usage limits, and AI agents show up on both sides of security and finance.

## Chip curbs slow Chinese labs as Nvidia pauses Southeast Asia shipments
Reuters reports Z.AI has slipped behind schedule on its next flagship model, and Moonshot AI expects gaps between major releases to stretch from six months to a year or more without overseas compute. Nvidia has told server partners to suspend shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. Why it matters: export rules now reach the offshore data centers Chinese labs relied on, putting existing builds and their financing at risk.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-november-27-2026/)

## OpenAI corrects a GPT-6.2 billing error and banks a usage reset
OpenAI says a billing mistake at the Nov 17 launch of GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol wrongly discounted most usage; limits now arrive 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol than last week. Go, Plus, Pro and Business subscribers get one banked reset and no charge for past usage. Why it matters: frontier pricing is still being tuned live, and tiered model families make the true cost per task hard to read.
Source: Sam on AI (https://samonai.substack.com/p/ai-brief-november-27-2026)

## Anthropic moves its Messages API from Python to Rust
Anthropic says the Rust rewrite uses about 70% less CPU per request and cuts median time to first token by about 20%, shipped with no downtime. Internal tests passed about 98%, but only about a quarter of real inputs matched, so it built live traffic shadowing to compare both versions. Why it matters: inference efficiency now comes from serving infrastructure as much as from the models themselves.
Source: Sam on AI (https://samonai.substack.com/p/ai-brief-november-27-2026)

## Microsoft tracks state-backed groups using the ARTEX agent framework
Microsoft Threat Intelligence says ARTEX, an open AI penetration-testing framework public since July 2026, is now used by groups including Storm-0940, Onyx Sleet and Salt Typhoon. One Storm-0940 campaign logged more than 151,000 failed sign-ins, peaking at 2,900 per minute. Why it matters: agentic tooling is lowering the cost of large-scale intrusion attempts for well-funded attackers.
Source: Sam on AI (https://samonai.substack.com/p/ai-brief-november-27-2026)

## Canada doubles its AI Compute Access Fund to C$1 billion
Ottawa added C$700 million to the fund, bringing it to C$1 billion. It will cover up to 80% of eligible compute costs for businesses buying from qualifying sovereign cloud providers. Why it matters: sovereign compute subsidies are becoming a standard industrial-policy lever outside the US and China.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-november-27-2026/)

## Sierra raises $1.8B Series F at a $30B valuation
Customer-service agent maker Sierra closed a $1.8 billion Series F on Nov 23, led by Greenoaks Capital and Lightspeed. A November tally counts 167 AI rounds so far, with OpenAI's $11.0B round the largest. Why it matters: investors are paying frontier-lab style valuations for applied agent companies with real revenue.
Source: AI Funding (https://aifunding.me/roundups/november-2026)

## Tencent open-sources DFly2 for faster inference
DFly2 drafts several tokens ahead for the main model to verify, a speculative decoding approach. Tencent reports up to 81% higher throughput for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 64,000 tokens of context and up to 4.2x faster Hy4 Flash agent tasks. Why it matters: open inference tricks help labs facing compute limits get more out of the chips they already have.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-november-27-2026/)

Trend watch: compute scarcity is the common thread, from export curbs and Canadian subsidies to Rust rewrites and speculative decoding that squeeze more work out of every chip.
