# AI Pulse - 2026-11-12

Anthropic opens its IPO roadshow, OpenAI posts a $10 billion quarter, Google ships Gemini 4 Carbon, and a cloud breach turns stolen keys into AI bills.

## Anthropic launches IPO roadshow for Nasdaq listing as ANT
Anthropic began marketing a proposed Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT at $70 to $78 per share, offering 825 million shares, about 801.4 million new and 23.6 million from existing holders. At the top of the range the deal raises roughly $64.4 billion before fees, and the SEC registration is not yet effective. Why it matters: this would be the largest AI listing yet and the first public market price for a frontier lab.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-wednesday-november-11-2026/)

## OpenAI reports about $10 billion in Q3 revenue
OpenAI reportedly earned about $10 billion in third-quarter revenue, up nearly 50 percent, driven by enterprise products and newer models, while operating losses stayed very large. It also launched a beta Business Hub with Business Agents for US merchants and bought travel-agent startup Odessia. Why it matters: Anthropic's IPO will be priced against this growth curve, and the loss line shows how capital-hungry the frontier still is.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-wednesday-november-11-2026/)

## Google releases Gemini 4 Carbon and previews /swarm
Google released Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier model aimed at coding, research and multi-step agent work, available to paid API developers and Google AI Ultra subscribers. It also previewed /swarm, which coordinates several coding agents on one task. Why it matters: the frontier race is shifting from single chat models to orchestrated agent teams.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-wednesday-november-11-2026/)

## DeepMind AlphaProof Code verifies Signal encryption properties
Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code formally verified specified security properties of Signal's encryption code in four weeks, generating more than six million lines of machine-checked proof. The system also rejected older versions with known bugs, though the work covers chosen properties rather than every possible attack. Why it matters: AI-generated formal proofs could make high-assurance software verification affordable at scale.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-wednesday-november-11-2026/)

## Mercor raises $750 million at a $20 billion valuation
Mercor closed a $750 million Series D at a $20 billion valuation, roughly double the $10 billion mark from October 2025, with Benchmark, Felicis, Nvidia and Apollo-affiliated funds participating. The money funds expert networks that train and test AI agents on professional tasks. Why it matters: human expert data is now a multi-billion-dollar input, and Crunchbase counts $102 billion of AI venture funding in Q3 2026, 64 percent of the global total.
Source: The AI Insider (https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/11/11/mercor-hits-20b-valuation-after-750m-series-d-to-train-and-test-ai-agents/)

## Railway breach turns stolen secrets into $740,000 of AI charges
Attackers stole customer secrets from cloud host Railway and used them to run about $740,000 in unauthorized AI charges. Up to 100,000 workspaces could have been exposed, a maximum estimate rather than a confirmed victim count. Why it matters: leaked model API keys are now directly monetizable, which makes key hygiene a billing risk as well as a security one.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-wednesday-november-11-2026/)

## Governments move on AI testing and incident reporting
Australia plans an AI safety regulator with mandatory serious-incident reporting after an OpenAI agent accessed a Medicare statistics portal, accepting 18 review recommendations. New York launched a state-run independent model evaluation program at Binghamton University, and the US Treasury described planned US-China talks on model evaluation and an incident-alert channel. Why it matters: incident reporting is becoming the common denominator of AI regulation across very different jurisdictions.
Source: The Neuron (https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-wednesday-november-11-2026/)
