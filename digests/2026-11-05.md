# AI Pulse - 2026-11-05

Compute deals and security research dominate a day when AI chip stocks gave back gains and China tightened its grip on cross-border deals.

## AMD agrees to buy inference-chip startup Taalas for about $2.9B
AMD disclosed in a filing that it will acquire Toronto-based Taalas in a cash-and-stock deal worth roughly $2.9 billion, of which about $1.4 billion is contingent on milestones. The deal still needs regulatory approval. Why it matters: AMD is buying specialised inference silicon to compete with Nvidia on the fastest-growing slice of AI compute spend.
Source: The Globe and Mail via AI Weekly (https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)

## Google backstops Anthropic's $19B Kentucky data center lease
Google will provide a credit backstop for about $19 billion in Anthropic lease obligations across three buildings on a 401MW campus in Hawesville, Kentucky. Why it matters: hyperscaler balance sheets are now underwriting frontier-lab capacity directly, tying Google more tightly to Anthropic's compute roadmap.
Source: Unite.AI via AI Weekly (https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)

## OpenAI prototype finds 948 Linux privilege-escalation bugs
An OpenAI research pipeline found 948 local privilege-escalation vulnerabilities with working proof-of-concept exploits in a stock Linux kernel, at a total API cost of about $1.21 million, or roughly $1,279 per unique bug. Separately, a Xen advisory credits Anthropic's Claude with two critical VM-escape flaws. Why it matters: automated vulnerability discovery has reached industrial scale, which raises the pressure on maintainers to patch as fast as models can find bugs.
Source: OpenAI and The Hacker News via AI Weekly (https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)

## China blocks SoftBank's $5.4B purchase of ABB Robotics
China's NDRC will not clear SoftBank's $5.4 billion acquisition of ABB's robotics division, and SoftBank shares fell about 5 percent on the news. Why it matters: physical-AI and robotics consolidation is now squarely inside the US-China regulatory crossfire, not just chips.
Source: Financial Times via AI Weekly (https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)

## Moonshot AI sought Nvidia GPUs in Europe amid US curbs
CNBC reports that China's Moonshot AI looked for access to Nvidia chips through European providers, with premiums of 30 to 50 percent reportedly floated, while Nvidia chips sit stockpiled in Hong Kong amid Chinese import restrictions. Why it matters: export controls are reshaping where Chinese labs train, and grey-market compute is getting more expensive rather than disappearing.
Source: CNBC (https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/moonshot-ai-nvidia-chips-europe-china.html)

## Funding: Sand.ai raises $200M, Sentra $150M, Pliant $80M
Video-model maker Sand.ai raised a $200 million Series A+, data-security firm Sentra closed a $150 million Series C led by Capital One Ventures to govern which people and AI agents can reach sensitive data, and Pliant raised an $80 million Series C for payment rails that AI agents can use. Why it matters: the money is following agents into the plumbing layer, security and payments, not just into models.
Source: Tech Startups (https://techstartups.com/2026/11/05/startup-funding-news-today-november-5-2026-bit-bio-sand-ai-sentra-pliant-more/)

## AI chip and data center stocks slide after the US election
Nvidia led a broad drop in AI chip stocks that erased the prior day's rally, while Hut 8 fell 23 percent and IREN 11 percent after the Democratic congressional sweep. Goldman Sachs warned that local moratoria could affect about 45 percent of planned US data center expansion. Why it matters: power and permitting politics are becoming a direct input to AI infrastructure valuations.
Source: Reuters via AI Weekly (https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)

Trend watch: compute is consolidating through acquisitions and balance-sheet guarantees while politics, in Washington and Beijing alike, increasingly decides where that compute can be built and who can buy it.
